\begin{abstract}
Expensive many-objective optimization requires using available samples to build
surrogate models and select promising candidate solutions under a limited budget
of expensive function evaluations. For relation-based surrogate models, quality
grouping based on a small set of representative solutions may not fully capture
quality differences within the population. Selecting candidates solely by relation
scores also makes limited use of classification ambiguity and performance
indicator information. To address these issues, we propose PACDIS, a
surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm that combines penalty-based boundary
intersection (PBI)-assisted quality classification (PAQC) with
criterion-diversified infill selection (CDIS). PAQC constructs reference directions
from the current nondominated solutions and computes continuous PBI quality scores.
It combines these scores with representative-based binary labels using weights
that vary with evaluation progress to form training groups for relation learning.
CDIS first screens candidates by relation quality, then applies ambiguity-oriented
or indicator-oriented ranking to select a batch of solutions for expensive
evaluation. The two strategies use classification ambiguity and predictions from
a separate indicator surrogate, respectively. Experiments on DTLZ and WFG test
problems with 10, 15, and 20 objectives show that PACDIS achieves competitive
optimization performance against six comparison algorithms under a limited
evaluation budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Stakes: expensive evaluations must support progress and multiple trade-offs.
Engineering applications such as airfoil design and power electronic circuit
optimization involve conflicting objectives whose evaluation can require costly
simulations~\cite{ref:gcs_pso,ref:emmoea}. The available evaluation budget limits
how many designs an optimizer can examine. In many-objective problems, this
restriction accompanies a selection difficulty: Pareto dominance provides less
discrimination as the number of objectives increases~\cite{ref:maoea_survey}.
Decomposition and reference-point methods help organize the search across
trade-offs~\cite{ref:moead,ref:nsga3}, but conventional evolutionary search still
requires objective evaluations for its new solutions. Expensive many-objective
optimization therefore requires selection rules that use scarce evaluations to
improve both convergence and the representation of different trade-offs.

% Surrogate targets: introduce the alternatives relevant to relation learning.
Surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) use predictions to screen
candidates before expensive evaluation~\cite{ref:jin_saea,ref:saea_survey}.
Learning a separate Kriging model for each objective makes the model count
grow with the number of objectives~\cite{ref:krvea}. Recent alternatives learn
convergence and diversity indicators, grid labels, or pairwise comparisons
that directly support selection~\cite{ref:hesea,ref:gcs_pso,ref:pcsaea}.

% Relation learning: identify the grouping and infill decisions.
The relation-learning algorithm REMO divides evaluated solutions into positive
and non-positive groups and trains a three-class model on ordered pairs from
these groups~\cite{ref:remo}. It aggregates predicted relations to rank
candidates for expensive evaluation. Its grouping rule defines the quality
information in the training pairs, while its infill rule determines how the
predictions guide evaluation. The relation-model framework of Hao et al. distinguishes data
preparation, model training, and model usage~\cite{ref:relation_framework}.

% First motivation: limited representative directions can restrict supervision.
First, representative-based grouping derives its directional supervision from
a small set of selected solutions. REMO selects representatives with the radial
space division-based evolutionary algorithm (RSEA), which uses a two-dimensional
radial grid~\cite{ref:remo,ref:rsea}. REMO's penalty-based boundary intersection
(PBI) comparisons use the original objective vectors, with the representatives
providing the assessment directions. A limited representative set may leave
directions in the current nondominated population without a separate quality
assessment. Solutions that share a representative-based label can differ in
quality along these additional directions. REMO gives pairs within each group
the same relation label, so these differences do not directly enter its training
targets.

% Second motivation: distinguish ranking quality from other infill information.
Second, a candidate's predicted rank and the decisiveness of its class
probabilities describe different properties. REMO ranks candidates by relation
scores, without a separate selection criterion for classification ambiguity or
an additional quality indicator~\cite{ref:remo}. Other infill methods use several
sources of information. The Kriging-assisted two-archive algorithm adapts
sampling to convergence, diversity, and uncertainty~\cite{ref:kta2}, while Qin
et al. select evaluations through expected improvement in a performance
indicator~\cite{ref:emmoea}.

% Overview: assign a distinct role to each component.
We develop PACDIS by modifying REMO's training-group construction and infill
selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). \emph{PBI-assisted quality classification}
(PAQC) defines the training groups, and \emph{criterion-diversified infill
selection} (CDIS) allocates expensive evaluations.

% Contributions: two concrete mechanisms, with experimental evidence below.
The two methodological contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Relation supervision using nondominated information.}
PAQC combines representative-based labels with continuous PBI quality scores to
determine the positive training group. Current nondominated solutions supply
the assessment directions. PAQC fuses the two signals into a
progress-dependent positive-group ranking.
\item \textbf{Infill criteria for relation-screened candidates.}
CDIS first screens candidates by relation quality, then applies either an
ambiguity-oriented or an indicator-oriented ranking. The former rewards less
decisive relation-class predictions; the latter uses a surrogate of a quality
indicator based on density and front shape~\cite{ref:sde,ref:piea}.
\end{enumerate}

% Results preview: descriptive means from the current FE500 table snapshot.
The experiments compare PACDIS with six SAEAs on 48 DTLZ and WFG configurations with 10,
15, and 20 objectives under a configured budget of 500 evaluations.
PACDIS achieves the lowest mean inverted generational distance plus (IGD$^+$)
in 11 configurations, including WFG2 and WFG7 at all three
objective counts (Tables~\ref{tab:exp:dtlz} and~\ref{tab:exp:wfg}).
Separate experiments compare algorithm variants and examine the probability
of selecting each infill mode
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp:ablation} and~\ref{sec:exp:pmix}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Surrogate Targets for Expensive Optimization}
\label{sec:related:saea}

% Modeling targets: establish alternatives to objective-wise regression.
The choice of prediction target determines which information a surrogate
provides for evolutionary selection. Objective-wise regression retains the
individual trade-offs: K-RVEA trains one Kriging model per objective and uses
reference vectors to guide the search~\cite{ref:krvea}. The Kriging-assisted
two-archive algorithm (KTA2) also approximates individual objectives and
maintains archives that emphasize convergence and
diversity~\cite{ref:kta2}. Such approaches require more models as the number
of objectives grows, and selection must combine their predictions and
uncertainty estimates. This motivates learning a smaller set of quantities
that directly support selection~\cite{ref:hesea}.

% Indicator surrogates already address model reduction; acknowledge them.
Indicator surrogates compress objective information into measures of solution
quality. The hierarchical and ensemble surrogate-assisted evolutionary
algorithm (HES-EA) learns convergence and diversity indicators through pairs
of models within a clustering framework~\cite{ref:hesea}. A cluster surrogate
routes candidates to the corresponding indicator models. The performance indicator-based
evolutionary algorithm (PIEA) selects among shift-based density estimation
(SDE), additive epsilon, and
Minkowski-distance indicators using historical feedback~\cite{ref:piea}.
It trains a surrogate for the selected indicator and combines its predictions
with distribution information during selection. These methods already provide alternatives to
regressing every objective; the relevant distinction is which quality
information the learning target retains. Classification and relation models
make a further choice: they encode quality through discrete labels or
comparisons~\cite{ref:csea,ref:remo}.

\subsection{Supervision for Classification and Relation Learning}
\label{sec:related:relation}

% Different label constructions preserve different objective-space information.
Classification-based selection depends on the rule that assigns quality
labels to evaluated solutions. CSEA constructs labels from comparisons with
reference solutions and uses a neural network to classify new
candidates~\cite{ref:csea}. Yuan and Banzhaf's dominance-prediction approach
trains separate classifiers for Pareto dominance and $\theta$-dominance,
combining their predictions in a two-stage preselection
strategy~\cite{ref:theta_dea_dp}. Grid classification-based surrogate-assisted
particle swarm optimization (GCS-PSO) instead predicts objective-space grid
locations, from which it assesses convergence and
diversity~\cite{ref:gcs_pso}. The supervision rule therefore determines which
directional or distribution information a classifier can learn.

% Closest relation methods: separate group construction from model use.
REMO constructs pairwise supervision from two groups of evaluated
solutions~\cite{ref:remo}. It uses representative-based PBI comparisons to
form the groups, trains a three-class relation model on ordered solution
pairs, and aggregates predicted relations into candidate scores. PC-SAEA
constructs comparison labels using a fitness assessment that considers
convergence and diversity; it also validates the model before deciding how
to use its predictions~\cite{ref:pcsaea}. The recent relation-model framework
of Hao et al. explicitly separates data preparation, model training, and
model usage~\cite{ref:relation_framework}. Within this framework, changing
the quality information that defines training pairs is a distinct design
choice from changing the classifier or its use in selection.

% PAQC positioning: supplement representative-induced directions, with bounds.
RSEA uses radial projection and adaptive grids for selection, with both
crowding and convergence considered to address the effects of curvature
information loss~\cite{ref:rsea}. In REMO, the resulting representatives
retain their original objective vectors and provide the directions for
PBI-based grouping~\cite{ref:remo}. A limited representative set may leave
some directions in the current nondominated population without a separate
quality assessment. PAQC supplements this grouping signal with PBI quality
information along directions derived from current nondominated objective
vectors, with uniform-direction fallbacks when that construction is
unavailable. Its progress-dependent fusion determines positive-group
membership while retaining REMO's three-class relation model
(Section~\ref{sec:method}). The distinction from dominance prediction and
grid classification therefore lies in how PAQC combines two sources of
directional information to construct relation-training groups.

\subsection{Infill Criteria for Expensive Evaluations}
\label{sec:related:selection}

% Existing uncertainty and switching mechanisms preclude broad novelty claims.
Infill selection determines which surrogate predictions lead to new
expensive evaluations. KTA2 adapts its sampling strategy to the current need
for convergence, diversity, or uncertainty reduction~\cite{ref:kta2}, while
CSEA combines predicted class membership with classification uncertainty
when selecting candidates~\cite{ref:csea}. For objectives with different
evaluation latencies, the asynchronous influence-based evolutionary
algorithm (AIEA) combines candidate influence with a most-uncertain-first
rule for choosing the next objective to evaluate~\cite{ref:aiea}. These
methods already use uncertainty and multiple selection considerations, but
their decisions concern different prediction targets and evaluation
settings. PACDIS considers a batch of solutions for evaluation on all
objectives, using relation predictions as the initial quality screen.

% Indicator infill is a close precedent; distinguish acquisition quantities.
Indicator-based infill provides a further way to combine convergence and
diversity in evaluation allocation. Qin et al. search with
objective-wise Gaussian process models, then use a separate indicator
model to select the candidate with the largest expected improvement in
that indicator~\cite{ref:emmoea}. This separates candidate generation from
the criterion for expensive evaluation. PC-SAEA uses validation reliability
to choose between direct, reversed,
and suspended use of its comparison model~\cite{ref:pcsaea}. Expected
improvement, validation reliability, and ambiguity in a candidate's class
probabilities quantify different properties: predicted gain, model-level
correctness, and confidence in individual classifications, respectively.

% CDIS positioning: identify the concrete inherited and modified operations.
CDIS retains relation-guided candidate generation and applies relation-quality
screening before either of its final selection criteria. Its
ambiguity-oriented mode rewards candidates whose relation-class predictions
are less decisive; its indicator-oriented mode reranks candidates with a
surrogate of a quality indicator that uses shift-based density estimation
and front-shape information~\cite{ref:sde,ref:piea}. Each iteration selects
one mode with a fixed probability when the indicator model is available,
and otherwise uses the ambiguity-oriented mode. Thus, CDIS specifies how
classification ambiguity and indicator assessment allocate evaluations
within a relation-screened pool. Together, PAQC and CDIS modify the
supervision and evaluation-allocation rules of REMO, building on established
relation learning and indicator-based selection.

\section{The Proposed PACDIS}
\label{sec:method}

PACDIS builds on relation-based surrogate-assisted optimisation, which uses a
classifier to predict the relative quality of solution pairs~\cite{ref:remo}.
It introduces two mechanisms. \emph{PBI-assisted quality classification}
(PAQC) combines REMO's representative-based labels~\cite{ref:remo} with PBI
scores along directions derived from the current nondominated set. Both signals
use the estimated ideal point as their origin, and their combination determines
the positive training group. \emph{Criterion-diversified infill selection}
(CDIS) generates candidates through relation-guided search and selects a batch
for expensive evaluation using either indicator-based reranking or
ambiguity-rewarded ranking. The algorithm retains the existing relation-learning,
indicator-assessment, and environmental-selection modules.

\subsection{Overall Framework}
\label{sec:method:framework}

We consider the expensive many-objective problem
$\min_{\vx\in\Omega}\vf(\vx)=[f_1(\vx),\ldots,f_M(\vx)]$,
where $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{D}$ and every evaluation of $\vf$ is
expensive. The number of real function evaluations is therefore limited to a
budget $FE_{\max}$. Let $FE$ be the number of
evaluations consumed so far and $t=FE/FE_{\max}\in[0,1]$ the evaluation
progress, which controls the hybrid score in PAQC.

PACDIS evaluates a Latin hypercube design of $N_{\mathrm{init}}$ solutions,
stores them in an archive $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{rc}}$, and repeats five steps
until it exhausts the budget. (i)~PAQC divides the current population
$\mathcal{P}$ into positive and non-positive groups and returns representatives
$\mathcal{R}$ for use as an additional mating pool.
(ii)~PACDIS generates ordered pairs from the groups and trains a three-class
relation network on their group labels. (iii)~It computes an indicator value
for each solution in $\mathcal{P}$ and fits a support vector regression model
with a radial basis function kernel (RBF-SVR) from decision vectors to indicator
values. (iv)~It draws a selection mode, accumulates
candidates through relation-guided search under that mode, and selects at most
$n_{\max}$ candidates. (v)~It evaluates the batch, appends it to
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{rc}}$, and applies RSEA's radial-grid environmental
selection~\cite{ref:rsea} to the whole archive to obtain the next population.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:framework} gives the procedure, with grouping and candidate
selection in Algorithms~\ref{alg:paqc} and~\ref{alg:cdis}.
The initial design satisfies $2\leq N_{\mathrm{init}}\leq FE_{\max}$, and $N$
is the target population size for environmental selection. The relation
surrogate $\mathcal{M}_R$ includes the group references and input normalisation
needed for prediction; $\mathcal{M}_I$ denotes the indicator surrogate when
available. The three procedures share these controls. Initialisation and
step~(v) consume the evaluation budget; PAQC and CDIS use evaluated data and
surrogate predictions. The remaining budget limits each batch.

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} shows the evaluation loop, PAQC group construction,
model training, and the two CDIS ranking branches.

\subsection{PBI-Assisted Quality Classification (PAQC)}
\label{sec:method:paqc}

REMO~\cite{ref:remo} constructs training groups using a small set of
representatives selected through RSEA's two-dimensional radial grid~\cite{ref:rsea}.
The representatives retain their original objective vectors, but their selection
depends on the projected distribution. This sparse summary may not fully reflect
the directional distribution of the current nondominated set in the original
many-objective space.

PAQC supplements the representative-based labels with PBI scores along
directions derived directly from current nondominated solutions. It combines
the two signals according to evaluation progress and assigns the highest-ranked
fraction to the positive group. The remaining solutions form its complement.

Figure~\ref{fig:paqc-mechanism} provides a geometric overview of these two
signals and their roles in grouping.

For PAQC, $\mathcal{P}=\{(\vx_i,\vf_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ is the
current population, with $N=|\mathcal{P}|$. We estimate the ideal point as the
component-wise population minimum $\vz^{*}=\min_i\vf_i$ and define
$\vy_i=\vf_i-\vz^{*}$. Both PAQC signals use this common displacement
coordinate system, with $\vz^{*}$ as its origin. We translate the objective
vectors without rescaling individual objectives.

\subsubsection{Quality Assessment Using Nondominated-Derived Directions}
\label{sec:method:paqc:cont}

The current nondominated solutions provide the additional directional information.
Let $\{\vf^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{ND}}}$ denote the objective vectors of
the first nondominated front, and let
$\mathcal{J}=\{j\mid\|\vf^{(j)}-\vz^{*}\|_2>0\}$.
PAQC normalises their nonzero displacements to construct the direction set:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vv_j&=\frac{\vf^{(j)}-\vz^{*}}{\|\vf^{(j)}-\vz^{*}\|_2},
\qquad j\in\mathcal{J},\\
\mathcal{V}&=\{\vv_j\mid j\in\mathcal{J}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:direction}
\end{equation}
Each direction defines a ray from $\vz^{*}$ through its source solution, so the
direction set follows the current nondominated distribution. PAQC uses uniform
reference directions when $M\leq3$, $N<50$, the nondominated set has too few
solutions or a degenerate objective range, or $\mathcal{J}$ is empty.

For $\vy_i\ne\boldsymbol{0}$, PAQC associates the solution with the direction
of largest cosine similarity,
$a(i)=\arg\max_j\vy_i^{\mathsf T}\vv_j/\|\vy_i\|_2$.
All directions have unit length. The conventional
PBI value~\cite{ref:moead} and its bounded PAQC quality signal are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d_{1,i}&=\vy_i^{\mathsf T}\vv_{a(i)},\\
d_{2,i}&=\|\vy_i-d_{1,i}\vv_{a(i)}\|_2,\\
g_i^{\mathrm{PBI}}&=d_{1,i}+\theta\,d_{2,i},\\
S_i&=\frac{1}{1+g_i^{\mathrm{PBI}}}\in(0,1],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pbi}
\end{equation}
where $\theta\geq0$ is the penalty parameter. A larger $S_i$ indicates a smaller
PBI value along the associated direction.

The population minimum gives
$\vy_i\geq\boldsymbol{0}$; both adaptive and uniform directions are
nonnegative, so $g_i^{\mathrm{PBI}}\geq0$ and $S_i\in(0,1]$.
For $\vy_i=\boldsymbol{0}$, PAQC assigns $d_{1,i}=d_{2,i}=0$ and $S_i=1$
directly, bypassing cosine association.

In the adaptive branch, each nondominated solution with nonzero displacement
aligns with its own direction in $\mathcal{V}$. Its perpendicular distance is
therefore zero, and \eqref{eq:pbi} reduces to
\begin{equation}
S_i=\frac{1}{1+\|\vf_i-\vz^{*}\|_2},
\qquad \vf_i\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{ND}}.
\label{eq:pbi-nd}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{ND}}$ denotes the current nondominated objective set.
On this set, $S_i$ ranks radial distance to $\vz^{*}$. For other solutions,
$d_{2,i}$ also penalises deviation from the associated direction.
The uniform-direction fallback uses the full PBI expression in \eqref{eq:pbi}.

\subsubsection{Representative-Based Grouping Signal}
\label{sec:method:paqc:coarse}

PAQC applies REMO's representative-based PBI classification rule~\cite{ref:remo}
in the displacement coordinate system. RSEA's radial-grid selection~\cite{ref:rsea}
provides $k$ evaluated representatives $\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{P}$ as
population-derived reference points. PAQC requests $k=\lceil1.5M\rceil$ representatives.

For each representative, define $\bar{\vr}_j=\vr_j-\vz^{*}$.
PAQC excludes zero-displacement representatives from directional association
and sets $\vw_j=\bar{\vr}_j/\|\bar{\vr}_j\|_2$ for the remaining ones.
For $\vy_i\ne\boldsymbol{0}$, it selects
$b(i)=\arg\max_j\vy_i^{\mathsf T}\vw_j/\|\vy_i\|_2$.
Using this associated direction, the two distances are
$\hat d_{1,i}=\vy_i^{\mathsf T}\vw_{b(i)}$ and
$\hat d_{2,i}=\|\vy_i-\hat d_{1,i}\vw_{b(i)}\|_2$.
The binary label is
\begin{equation}
L_i=\mathbb{I}\!\left[
\frac{\hat d_{1,i}+\delta\,\hat d_{2,i}}
     {\|\vr_{b(i)}-\vz^{*}\|_2}\leq1\right]\in\{0,1\}.
\label{eq:label-bin}
\end{equation}
Normalising by $\|\vr_{b(i)}-\vz^{*}\|_2$ makes the associated representative
itself the threshold, so $L_i$ records on which side of the surface through that
representative a solution lies. This normalisation requires
$\vr_{b(i)}\ne\vz^{*}$. PAQC assigns $L_i=1$ directly when
$\vy_i=\boldsymbol{0}$. If no representative has nonzero displacement,
it sets $L_i=\mathbb{I}[\|\vy_i\|_2=0]$ and bypasses directional classification.
Otherwise, bounded bisection adjusts the signed class-balance coefficient
$\delta\in[-20,20]$ toward a positive-label fraction in $[0.3,0.7]$,
stopping when it reaches that interval or the search bracket is narrower than
$0.1$.

\subsubsection{Fusing Continuous Quality Scores with Representative-Based Labels}
\label{sec:method:paqc:hybrid}

With $t=FE/FE_{\max}$, PAQC combines the
continuous scores and binary labels to define the groups:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_i&=(1-t)S_i+tL_i,\\
\mathcal{C}_1&=\bigl\{\vx_i\in\mathcal{P}\;\big|\;
  \operatorname{rank}_{\downarrow}(H_i)\leq\lceil N/4\rceil\bigr\},\\
\mathcal{C}_2&=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{C}_1 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:group}
\end{equation}
The top quarter under $H_i$ forms the positive group, with $\mathcal{C}_2$
containing the remaining solutions. As $FE$ increases, the weight shifts from
directional quality to representative-based labels. Changes in group membership
change the ordered pairs used to train the relation model.
Section~\ref{sec:exp:ggp} examines the current convergence of the positive groups.

In Figure~\ref{fig:paqc-mechanism}(a), $R_1$ and $R_2$ define the local
thresholds for $L_i$. In panel~(b), $P_1$ and $P_2$ supply directions
$\vv_1$ and $\vv_2$ for $S_i$ through \eqref{eq:direction}. The reassignment
of $A$, $B$, and $C$ illustrates how the ranking in \eqref{eq:group} changes
group membership while retaining the representative-based thresholds.
The two-objective diagram uses $O=Z$ as the origin of displacement coordinates. Its PF is an
external reference; PAQC constructs directions from current nondominated
solutions. Membership in $\mathcal{C}_1$ depends on the fused ranking.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:paqc} summarises the construction. Population order resolves
ties in $H_i$, giving exactly $\lceil N/4\rceil$ positive-group members.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PBIQualityClassification (PAQC)}
\label{alg:paqc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require evaluated population $\mathcal{P}$, progress $t$
\Ensure groups $\mathcal{C}_1,\mathcal{C}_2$ and representatives $\mathcal{R}$
\State $\vz^{*}\gets\min_i\vf_i$; $\vy_i\gets\vf_i-\vz^{*}$
\State Construct $\mathcal{V}$ from nonzero nondominated displacements
\State Use uniform directions when the adaptive construction is unavailable
\State Associate nonzero $\vy_i$ with $\mathcal{V}$ by cosine similarity
\State Compute $S_i$ by \eqref{eq:pbi} for nonzero displacements
\State Assign $S_i\gets1$ at the ideal point
\State Select $k$ representatives $\mathcal{R}$ by radial-grid selection
\State Form representative directions from $\vr_j-\vz^{*}$
\State Associate nonzero $\vy_i$ with valid representative directions
\State Obtain $L_i$ by \eqref{eq:label-bin}, with bounded bisection for $\delta$ and the stated zero-displacement rules
\State Fuse the two signals: $H_i\gets(1-t)S_i+tL_i$
\State $\mathcal{C}_1\gets$ the top $\lceil|\mathcal{P}|/4\rceil$ solutions under $H_i$
\State $\mathcal{C}_2\gets\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{C}_1$
\State \Return $(\mathcal{C}_1,\mathcal{C}_2,\mathcal{R})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}



\subsection{Relation Learning on the PAQC Groups}
\label{sec:method:relation}

PACDIS trains REMO's relation model~\cite{ref:remo} on the PAQC groups.
For each ordered pair of distinct solutions, it concatenates the decision
vectors into an input $[\vx_i,\vx_j]\in\mathbb{R}^{2D}$ and assigns the label

\begin{equation}
y_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
+1, & \vx_i\in\mathcal{C}_1,\ \vx_j\in\mathcal{C}_2,\\
-1, & \vx_i\in\mathcal{C}_2,\ \vx_j\in\mathcal{C}_1,\\
0,  & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:pairlabel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_1,\mathcal{C}_2$ follow \eqref{eq:group}. These labels
encode group membership.
PAQC randomly subsamples same-group pairs towards the size of each cross-group
class to balance the three labels.

We employ the same relation-learning architecture as REMO: min--max normalised
pair inputs, a class-balanced training/held-out split, and a feed-forward pattern
network ending in a softmax over the three labels.
For a pair, the network outputs
$\bm{\pi}(\vx_a,\vx_b)=[\pi_{+1},\pi_0,\pi_{-1}]$, the predicted probabilities
that the first solution belongs to the higher group, that both belong to the
same group, and that the second belongs to the higher group. The training procedure retains a held-out partition.

For an unevaluated candidate $\vx$, the trained network predicts the four
families of ordered comparisons $(\mathcal{C}_1,\vx)$, $(\vx,\mathcal{C}_1)$,
$(\mathcal{C}_2,\vx)$ and $(\vx,\mathcal{C}_2)$, that is on $2N$ pairs per
candidate. With $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}$ the family-wise
mean probability vectors in that order, the relation score aggregates the
evidence that $\vx$ is not inferior to the positive group and is superior to the
non-positive group, minus the opposite evidence,
\begin{equation}
R(\vx)=2\bigl[c_{-1}(\vx)+d_{+1}(\vx)-a_{+1}(\vx)-b_{-1}(\vx)\bigr]
\in[-4,4],
\label{eq:relscore}
\end{equation}
the bound following from each probability vector summing to one. $R(\vx)$ is the
aggregate group-relative preference used to order unevaluated candidates.

\subsection{Criterion-Diversified Infill Selection (CDIS)}
\label{sec:method:cdis}

CDIS first screens candidates by relation quality, then ranks the retained
subset using either classification ambiguity together with relation scores or
predictions from a separate indicator surrogate. It selects a batch under a
fixed evaluation cap. The actual batch size $n_b$ also depends on candidate
availability and the remaining budget (Section~\ref{sec:method:cdis:mix}).

Both modes use the same pool-construction procedure. Genetic
variation acts on the current population and representatives $\mathcal{R}$.
The relation score in \eqref{eq:relscore} ranks the offspring; up to
$|\mathcal{R}|$ of the best join $\mathcal{R}$ as parents for the next inner
generation. The search stops when the cumulative number of generated candidates
reaches $g_{\max}$, then deduplicates all generated candidates to form
$\mathcal{A}$. Mode-specific aggregation can change the inner rankings and
hence the realized pool.

\subsubsection{Ambiguity-Rewarded Infill}
\label{sec:method:cdis:exp}

The ambiguity-oriented mode first applies a relation-quality screen and then
ranks the retained candidates using predicted quality and classification
ambiguity. Its relation score uses the
largest softmax class probability as the weight of each ordered pair in the
family-wise averages of \eqref{eq:relscore}. This pair-level weighting gives
less influence to comparisons with lower maximum class probabilities when
estimating candidate quality.

Let $q=0.30$ be the nominal fraction retained by relation-quality screening
in either mode. Candidates whose relation scores are at or above the
$(1-q)$-quantile of $R(\mathcal{A})$ form $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{amb}}$.
In the absence of ties, this screen retains approximately the highest-scoring
30\% of the pool; ties can increase that fraction.

To distinguish candidates within the retained set, let $\mathcal{O}(\vx)$
denote all ordered comparisons between candidate $\vx$ and the positive and
non-positive training groups. Let $\pi_k(\vu,\vv)$ be the predicted
probability of relation class $k\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ for an ordered pair
$(\vu,\vv)$. The candidate-level ambiguity and the acquisition score within
$\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{amb}}$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U(\vx)&=1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}(\vx)|}
  \sum_{(\vu,\vv)\in\mathcal{O}(\vx)}
  \max_{k\in\{-1,0,+1\}}\pi_k(\vu,\vv),\\
A_{\mathrm{amb}}(\vx)&=\widetilde{R}(\vx)+\lambda\,\widetilde{U}(\vx),
  \qquad \lambda=0.30.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:expscore}
\end{equation}
Here tildes denote min--max normalization within
$\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{amb}}$, with a constant quantity assigned the normalized
value $0.5$. The ambiguity-oriented branch returns
the first $n_b$ candidates in descending $A_{\mathrm{amb}}$ order, preserving
candidate order for exact ties.

\subsubsection{Indicator-Based Infill}
\label{sec:method:cdis:ind}

CDIS uses a single SDE-based quality indicator~\cite{ref:sde,ref:piea}.
SDE~\cite{ref:sde} evaluates a solution
through distances to neighboring objective vectors after shifting each
neighbor's better objective components to the target solution's values.
We scale the SDE scores and refine near-zero values with a scaled negative
$L_p$ distance to the current ideal point~\cite{ref:piea}.
We estimate $p$ from the dispersion of normalized $L_p$ distances among
current nondominated solutions~\cite{ref:tworea,ref:piea}.
The indicator maps the combined scores to a bounded scale, with larger
values indicating higher quality.

The indicator supplies the scalar quality target $I_i$.
We compute $I_i$ for each solution in the
current population and train an
RBF-SVR, denoted by $\mathcal{M}_I$, on
$\mathcal{D}=\{(\vx_i,I_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$; we denote its prediction by
$\widehat I(\vx)=\mathcal{M}_I(\vx;\mathcal{D})$.
The relation score first retains the top
$\lceil q|\mathcal{A}|\rceil$ candidates to form $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{ind}}$.
In this mode the family-wise relation probabilities are arithmetic averages.
CDIS then ranks the screened candidates by descending $\widehat I$ and
selects up to $n_{\max}$ directly. Failed or nonfinite indicator predictions
fall back to relation-score ranking.

\subsubsection{Selection-Criterion Switching}
\label{sec:method:cdis:mix}

Let $m\in\{\mathrm{amb},\mathrm{ind}\}$ denote the mode. With $u\sim U[0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
m=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{ind}, & \text{if the indicator surrogate is available}\\
              & \text{and }u<p_{\mathrm{mix}},\\[2pt]
\mathrm{amb}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:mode}
\end{equation}
where the probability $p_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.5$ stays fixed. Each iteration uses one ranking
criterion, with the ambiguity-oriented mode as the fallback when the indicator
surrogate is unavailable. Let $\mathcal{H}$ denote the retained set selected by
the current mode. CDIS selects candidates in the current mode's order,
truncating the batch to $n_{\max}=6$ and the remaining budget; if the pool is
empty, genetic variation supplies fallback candidates.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cdis} states the main selection procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DiversifiedInfillSelection (CDIS)}
\label{alg:cdis}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require population $\mathcal{P}$, representatives $\mathcal{R}$,
         surrogates $\mathcal{M}_R,\mathcal{M}_I$, mode $m$
\Ensure ordered candidate batch $\mathcal{S}$
\State Run relation-guided variation from $\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{R}$ using mode $m$
\State $\mathcal{A}\gets$ the accumulated, deduplicated candidate pool
\If{$m=\mathrm{ind}$}
  \State $\mathcal{H}\gets$ top $q$ fraction by $R$
  \State Rerank $\mathcal{H}$ by $\widehat I$, falling back to $R$ if needed
\Else
  \State $\mathcal{H}\gets\{\,\vx\in\mathcal{A}:R(\vx)\geq Q_{1-q}(R(\mathcal{A}))\,\}$
  \State Compute $A_{\mathrm{amb}}$ within $\mathcal{H}$ by \eqref{eq:expscore}
\EndIf
\State $\mathcal{S}\gets$ the first $n_b$ candidates in $\mathcal{H}$ under the selected ranking
\State \Return $\mathcal{S}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Computational Complexity}
\label{sec:method:complexity}

Let $N$ be the population size, $N_v=O(N)$ the number of directions, $N_c$ the
size of the deduplicated candidate pool, $D$ the number of decision variables
and $M$ the number of objectives.

In PAQC, association and the continuous PBI computation in \eqref{eq:pbi}
cost $O(NN_vM)$. Constructing \eqref{eq:direction} requires one normalisation
of the nondominated vectors. Bounded bisection for \eqref{eq:label-bin} costs
$O(B_\delta NkM)$, where the bracket and tolerance fix $B_\delta$.
Representative selection adds nondominated sorting and radial-projection
distances. With $N_v=O(N)$ and $k=O(N)$, the dominant cost is $O(N^2M)$.
Generating relation pairs adds $O(N^2D)$.

In CDIS, the following per-iteration costs exclude surrogate fitting.
Scoring one candidate requires $2N$ network inputs of
dimension $2D$. Constructing the inputs for the pool costs $O(N_cND)$.
The relation network has a fixed number of dense layers with hidden-layer widths
proportional to $D$, so predicting one ordered pair costs $O(D^2)$.
Relation prediction over the pool therefore costs $O(N_cND^2)$.
The indicator fitness costs $O(N^2M)$; the indicator-oriented mode also
predicts indicator values on the screened subset. Sorting the ambiguity-rewarded acquisition
costs $O(N_c\log N_c)$.
The total is $O(N_cND^2+N^2M+N_c\log N_c)$.

\subsection{Experimental Settings}
\label{sec:exp:settings}

\paragraph{Test problems and evaluation budget}
The main comparison covers DTLZ1--DTLZ7 and WFG1--WFG9 with
$M\in\{10,15,20\}$, giving 48 problem--objective configurations.
We use PlatEMO~\cite{ref:platemo} with a configured population size of $N=100$
and a maximum budget of $FE_{\max}=500$ real function evaluations, including
initialization. The decision dimension is $D=30$, except for WFG2 and WFG3
at $M=10$ and $M=20$, whose definitions require $D=31$.
All 15-objective instances use $D=30$.

\paragraph{Compared algorithms}
We compare PACDIS with six surrogate-assisted algorithms:
REMO~\cite{ref:remo}, SSDE~\cite{ref:ssde}, PC-SAEA~\cite{ref:pcsaea},
PIEA~\cite{ref:piea}, CSEA~\cite{ref:csea}, and
HES-EA~\cite{ref:hesea}. REMO learns pairwise relations, SSDE uses a
self-organizing surrogate, and PC-SAEA uses pairwise comparison. PIEA learns
the selected performance indicator with support vector regression and uses
historical feedback to update indicator-selection probabilities.
CSEA uses classification, and HES-EA combines hierarchical and ensemble
surrogates with model reduction. Section~\ref{sec:exp:comparison} reports
the FE start point of each implementation.

\paragraph{PACDIS parameters}
We use the same configuration for all three objective counts:
$g_{\max}=3000$, $p_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.50$, and the nominal relation-screening
fraction $q=0.30$. The fixed internal constants are the ambiguity weight
$\lambda=0.30$ and continuous-PBI penalty $\theta=5$.
The requested representative count is
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{eff}}=\min\{N,\max(6,\lceil1.5M\rceil)\},
\label{eq:keff}
\end{equation}
giving 15, 23 and 30 at $M=10$, 15 and 20 with $N=100$.

\paragraph{Performance indicator and statistical reporting}
We measure performance by inverted generational distance plus (IGD$^+$);
smaller values indicate a better approximation to the reference Pareto front.
Tables~\ref{tab:exp:dtlz} and~\ref{tab:exp:wfg} report 20 independent runs per
entry from the final experiment workbooks. Each problem has three rows for
$M=10$, 15 and 20. Entries show the mean above the standard deviation in
parentheses, with light gray shading for the lowest mean among the seven
algorithms.

The statistical symbols follow unpaired rank-sum tests at $p<0.05$ on the
reported runs. Relative to PACDIS,
$+$ denotes a better baseline result, $-$ a worse result, and $=$ no detected
difference. The bottom rows count these pairwise outcomes in $+/-/=$ order
across the problem--objective configurations in each table.

\paragraph{Reference sets and execution environment}
We use PlatEMO's default DTLZ and WFG problem definitions with the $M$ and $D$
settings above. PlatEMO constructs each reference set through the problem's
\texttt{GetOptimum(10000)} method. The \texttt{IGDp} implementation computes
distances in the original objective space without additional normalization.
Most runs use MATLAB R2023a on a Windows workstation with an Intel Core
i5-12400 processor and 32 GB RAM. Six parallel workers execute independent
optimization runs.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:exp:ablation}

The ablation compares PACDIS (Full) with two deletion controls.
The w/o PAQC control uses REMO's representative-based PBI
classification in place of PAQC's fused group construction. The REMO
classifier adapts its PBI threshold, giving a variable positive-group size;
Full uses the fixed grouping rule in \eqref{eq:group}. This control retains the indicator model and
criterion switching.

The w/o CDIS control retains PAQC and replaces CDIS with REMO's original
relation-guided candidate module. It ranks candidates solely by relation score,

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We proposed PACDIS, a surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm for expensive
many-objective optimization. PACDIS introduces PBI-assisted quality classification
(PAQC) and criterion-diversified infill selection (CDIS) into REMO's relation-learning
framework. PAQC combines representative-based binary labels with continuous PBI
quality scores derived from the current nondominated solutions to form training
groups for relation learning. After screening candidates by relation quality,
CDIS uses ambiguity-oriented or indicator-oriented ranking to select solutions
for expensive evaluation. Experiments on DTLZ and WFG test suites show that
PACDIS achieves competitive optimization performance under a limited evaluation
budget. The ablation results further show that the full algorithm outperforms
variants that remove PAQC or CDIS on most test configurations.

PACDIS still has room for improvement on some test problems. Future work will
examine how candidate generation and screening affect Pareto front coverage and
explore infill selection strategies that adapt to changes in the population.
